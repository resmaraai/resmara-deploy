# Engine 4 brain: Campaign build

**Status:** greenlit by Adam (22 Nov) · **Builds in:** PR #26 · **Started:** 22 Nov 2026
**Existing spec:** `engine-specs/resmara-post-engine-spec.md` (14 Nov: plan, layouts, Art Director check, edit log, learner). This brain sets the direction; where they differ, this brain wins.

---

## 1. Job

Turn the home's file (Engine 3) and the agent's brand (Engine 2) into **14 one-of-a-kind posts
and the ad**, in that agent's look, telling the story of that home, so the agent can breeze
through, approve and go. Every campaign should look like *them*, and each one should land closer
to what they like than the last.

**Starts:** Engine 3 hands over the home's file (address, 14+ scored photos, video, facts).
**Ends:** the campaign is built and waiting behind pay to unlock (sign-up tour engine, section 5).

**Who it's for (Adam, 22 Nov):** the agent who doesn't want to think about it. Log in, paste a
link, it's all built; set the budget, choose Run it until it sells or not, breeze through the
posts, press go. Anyone who wants to dial in can change any photo, text or post. The build is a
big starting point, never a chore.

**Principle: never one template.** AI is involved in every decision, and no two agents get the
same campaign.

## 2. What it pulls from

1. **The brand kit** (Engine 2): logo, fonts, colours, Voice of the client.
2. **The home's file** (Engine 3): facts with sources, the scored photos, the video.
3. **The agent's style profile** (new, section 5): what they've liked, disliked, kept and changed,
   and what performed.
4. **The trend brief** (section 3): what's working on social right now, locally and nationally.

## 3. The trend brief: what's working right now

- **Local:** the top agents, teams and brokerages in the agent's market, judged on how their
  static posts actually perform (likes, comments, views). Meta's Business Discovery lets the app
  read other professional Instagram accounts' public metrics.
- **National:** the real estate brands and creators doing static posts best.
- **Output:** a short synopsis of the formats that are working (for example "full-bleed photo with
  a short overlaid headline outperforms boxed text; collages work for kitchens and outdoor
  spaces; story captions beat feature lists").
- **Shared per market, refreshed weekly**, not run on every build (cost and speed).
- **Learn formats, never copy:** no one else's designs, wording or photos.

## 4. Post styles, not themes (Adam, 22 Nov)

**No Modern / Classic / Rustic / Match my brand choice for the agent**, and the **Your look**
picker comes off My Brand (Adam, 22 Nov; this replaces Engine 2's "Look defaults to Match my
brand"). Instead the engine works
from a set of **post styles**, each rendered in the agent's brand:

- full photo with overlaid text on a soft gradient
- framed or boxed text
- collage (2–4 photos)
- minimal editorial
- bold typographic
- magazine cover

Within a style, the AI can lean more modern or more classic to suit the brand.

- **First campaign:** no up-front taste quiz. The build mixes the styles the trend brief rates
  highest that suit their brand, so the agent's reactions teach the engine fast.
- **Every later campaign** leans toward what they liked and kept, with fresh stories and enough
  variety that the feed never looks copy-pasted.

## 5. Learning the agent

- **👍 / 👎 on every post, always** ("Like this style?"), on the Gallery cards and inside each post.
  One tap, no explanations. This is the main signal.
- **A 👎 is just a tap, no reason asked** (Adam, 22 Nov). The engine works out why: every post keeps
  a record of its features (style, layout, how the name and details are shown, photo and room, how
  much text, colour treatment), and the AI compares what they 👎 with what they 👍 to find the
  pattern ("thumbs down on 4 of 5 posts with a boxed name bar"). Patterns it finds feed the next
  campaign and show in "What Resmara learned about you".
- **Approved untouched** counts as a like. **Edits** count too: a photo swap, rewritten text, a
  replaced post (the spec's edit log already records these).
- **Results:** the posts that got the most engagement (Engine 6) steer the next campaign.
- **The style profile** stores it all per agent. "What Resmara learned about you" on My Brand shows
  it in plain words ("You like overlaid text, no boxes, short headlines") so they can correct it.

## 5a. Most agents won't give feedback (Adam, 22 Nov)

Many will never tap 👍 or 👎: they'll like the first run, press go, and do the same next time. So
**quality can't depend on feedback.** Every post has to be super high quality on its own:

- **The quality bar is built in, not learned:** the design rulebook, the Art Director check (score
  every post; redo weak ones) and the checker (exact words, the agent's details, the real photo).
- **The gold set:** before launch, Adam rates a batch of generated posts; that set is what the Art
  Director check is tuned to, so "good enough" means Adam's standard.
- **Silence still teaches a little:** a campaign approved untouched counts as a like for everything
  in it, and results (Engine 6) still steer the next one.
- **Variety still applies** (section 9): even with no feedback, campaigns don't repeat.

## 6. The story (arc approved by Adam, 22 Nov)

| Days | Post |
|---|---|
| 1 | Just listed: the hero exterior, or the listing video if there is one |
| 2 | Highlights: the 3–4 things that make this home special |
| 3–6 | Room spotlights: kitchen, living, primary suite, the standout feature (pool, view, shop) |
| 7 | Report day: a lifestyle moment |
| 8 | The neighbourhood: local spots, schools, commute (from research) |
| 9 | A note from the agent, in their voice |
| 10–11 | More spotlights: outdoor space, details, a collage |
| 12 | Question post, to drive comments |
| 13 | Open house or showing invite if one is set; otherwise "last chance to see it this week" |
| 14 | Final spotlight, and the report |

The AI fits the arc to each home (no pool, no spotlight on a pool). The ad runs all 14 days: the
listing video if there is one, otherwise a carousel of the best photos, pointing to the listing page.

The posts tell that story: short words on the image, and the caption
tells the story in the agent's Voice of the client. Facts marked "please check" (Engine 3) are
never stated as fact. Compliance lines (license, brokerage) go on every post.

## 7. Photos

The scored photos (Engine 3) are matched to the story: the best photo for each day, no room
repeated on consecutive days, feature showcases (pool, view, shop), and text only where the photo
has calm space, never over the subject.

## 8. The calendar: two views (Adam, 22 Nov)

- **Calendar:** the month view, as today.
- **Gallery:** the 14 posts as bigger cards, with the design, the day and time, the title, the
  caption, and 👍 / 👎. Click a card to open the post.

## 9. How the posts get drawn

**The goal (Adam, 22 Nov): whatever looks coolest and best for the client.** Posts people stop on
while scrolling, that make the agent proud to share them.

**The image AI designs each post** (as the Boost and the homepage samples do): it lays the agent's
real photo, their fonts and colours, the words and the layout into one finished, magazine-quality
post. This gives the richest, most striking results.

**Keeping it consistent: the solution already designed in the Boost** (Post spec P8 and P9):

1. **Their own posts as references.** Each design call includes up to two of the agent's earlier
   posts, with the instruction to match their typography, type sizes, spacing and footer closely so
   the post reads as part of the same series, but never copy their photos, layout or words. From
   now on, those references are **the posts they gave a 👍** (then posts they kept untouched).
2. **The agent's details on every post:** see section 9a.
3. **The checker:** a second AI re-reads every finished post against the exact words, digits and
   the original photo. A failure is redone once with the reasons; if it still fails, it's flagged
   "Needs a look" in review.
4. **The Art Director check** (spec section 7): scores each post for layout, legibility and text
   over the subject, and redoes weak ones.

**Never in a rut (Adam, 22 Nov).** Learning must not narrow every campaign into the same post:

- **References set the brand feel, not the layout:** their typography, colour handling and spacing.
  The layout, crop and composition are chosen fresh for each post and each photo.
- **References rotate:** drawn from the pool of everything they've given a 👍, never the same two.
- **About two thirds familiar, one third new:** each campaign mostly uses styles they've liked, plus
  a few styles they haven't seen yet, chosen from the current trend brief. Their 👍 / 👎 on the new
  ones keeps the learning moving.
- **No repeats:** no layout twice in a row, and no campaign repeats the last campaign's sequence.
- **Trends move weekly,** so the "new" third keeps changing.

**No two agents alike in the same market (Adam, 22 Nov).** Each agent's references are only their
own posts; their brand kit (fonts, colours, voice) is theirs; and before building, the engine
checks the styles and signature treatments other Resmara agents in the same market have used in
their recent campaigns and avoids matching them.

**The fallback:** if lettering errors stay too frequent in testing, switch to the hybrid already in
the spec (section 7.4): the AI composes the image and placement, code renders the text in the
exact fonts.

## 9a. The agent's details: always there, never the same bar (Adam, 22 Nov)

- **Required on everything:** the agent's name, license number and phone number (plus the
  brokerage, per their rules). On the graphic, in the caption, or both, but always present. The
  checker confirms it on every post.
- **How it's shown varies,** like any other design choice: a footer bar, a small signature line, a
  corner mark, built into the layout, or carried in the caption. Not the same bar on every post for
  every client.
- **It's learned like everything else:** treatments on posts they 👍 are used more; on posts they 👎,
  less.

## 9b. The ad (built here, launched by Engine 6)

Engine 4 builds everything the ad needs; Engine 6 (Active campaign) puts it live in Meta, watches
it and reports on it. Full detail: `engine-specs/resmara-ad-engine-spec.md` (v1.3); the changes
since are noted below.

**What the agent sees:** the ad as a bar across the 14 days on the calendar; click it to flip
through the cards (or see the video) and edit the title and caption. The only ad question is the
budget, pre-filled at **$50 for the whole 14 days**, confirmed in launch details.

**The ad is built differently from the posts (Adam, 22 Nov).** Posts tell the story; the ad's only
job is to **stop the scroll and get the click**. So:

- **The hook first.** The AI looks at the home's file and the scored photos and finds what's most
  valuable or unusual about this home (the pool, the view, the kitchen, the shop, the light), then
  writes a short, curiosity-driving hook around it: "Have you seen a pool like this?", "This kitchen
  doesn't look like the rest of the house." **Always true to the house:** every hook must be backed
  by the home's file and the photo it sits on; no false or unverifiable claims ("best view in
  Redding", "nothing like it anywhere"); questions and invitations are fine. Fair Housing rules still
  apply in full (it's the law, not a style choice).
- **Its own graphic, minimal text.** The hook as a few big words over the strongest photo, plus the
  agent's details. Not one of the 14 posts reused; simple and instantly understood on any placement.
- **Listing video found → video ad and static ads, always both** (Adam, 22 Nov). The video ad (with
  the hook as its opening text) runs in the same ad set as the static hook ads, so Meta shows both
  and spends where people click. If Meta can't process the video, the static ads run alone. No
  video → the static hook ads.
- **Hooks are tested and learned.** Each ad goes out with **up to 3 hook versions** in the same ad
  set and budget; Meta shifts the spend to the one people click. Every result goes into a **hook
  library** across all Resmara agents (which hooks win for pools, views, kitchens, by market), so
  the hooks get sharper with every campaign.
- **Sizes for every placement:** 4:5 for feeds and 9:16 for Stories and Reels.
- **The copy around it is short:** primary text ≤ 125 characters, headline ≤ 40, button **Learn more**.

**The platform rulebook guides every ad decision (Adam, 22 Nov).** The ad is designed *to* the
platforms' rules, not checked against them afterwards:

- **The rulebook:** Meta's Advertising Standards (which cover Facebook, Instagram, Marketplace and
  Messenger), the housing Special Ad Category rules, Fair Housing law, and each placement's specs and
  safe zones. **Researched and refreshed monthly**, and immediately whenever Meta rejects or limits a
  Resmara ad, so it follows Meta's changes.
- **What it covers today (checked 22 Nov 2026):**
  - **Text on the image:** Meta retired its old 20% rule, so it no longer rejects ads for image text,
    but it still says ads with less text perform better, and text-heavy images can get less delivery
    and cost more. So a few big words, no more; the message lives in the ad text.
  - **Personal attributes:** ads must not assert or imply things about the viewer (their finances,
    family, age, religion and so on). "Have you seen a pool like this?" is fine; "Are you a first-time
    buyer?" or "Perfect for your growing family" is not (and the second also breaks Fair Housing).
  - **No misleading claims:** Meta now reviews text, images, video and the landing page together;
    every claim must be true and backed by the home's file.
  - **Placement specs:** 4:5 for feeds, 9:16 for Stories and Reels with the words kept out of the
    top and bottom areas the app covers.
- **The ad-policy check:** before launch, a checker reviews each ad (image, words, landing page)
  against the rulebook and fixes or flags anything at risk.
- **If Meta rejects or limits an ad anyway:** read Meta's reason, fix it, resubmit once
  automatically, tell the agent only if it still fails, and add the lesson to the rulebook.

**Fixed in code, never left to the AI** (Meta's housing rules, verified 22 Nov 2026)
- **Special Ad Category: HOUSING** on every campaign. Under it Meta fixes age at 18–65+, removes
  gender, ZIP code, exclusion and lookalike targeting, and requires location targeting of at least
  a 15-mile radius in the US.
- **Location:** the listing's city, or the address, with a 15-mile radius.
- **Objective:** traffic (link clicks). **Placements:** everywhere Meta places housing ads: the
  Facebook and Instagram feeds, Marketplace, Stories and Reels (Adam, 22 Nov).
- **Budget:** a **lifetime** budget of the agent's confirmed amount (never a daily budget: today's
  code sets $50 a day, about $700 over 14 days, and must be fixed first). Floor: Meta's minimum.
- **Schedule:** starts the go-live date at 8:00 AM local, ends 14 days later.
- **One campaign, one ad set, up to 3 hook versions**, named `<street> · Resmara · <round>`. Never two at once for the
  same listing.
- **Runs as the agent:** their Facebook Page and Instagram account on the ad.
- **Every link tagged** `utm_source=resmara&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign=<listing>`.

**What has to be true for the ad to run** (checked before launch; if not, the posts still go out
and the ad waits, with one clear fix)
- The agent's ad account is connected and active, with a payment method in Meta (Engine 1's setup
  plan walks them through it). Ad spend is billed by Meta to the agent; it's never part of the $49.
- Their Facebook Page is connected (and Instagram, for the Instagram placement).
- Resmara's Meta app has `ads_management` approved, and Resmara's business is verified.

**Where clicks go (Adam, 22 Nov):** the agent's Resmara listing page (Engine 5), which captures
leads and tracks visits. Until Engine 5 is live, the listing link they pasted.

**Changed since the ad spec (22 Nov):** the ad has its own hook graphic (not ~5 reused posts), up to
3 hook versions, all placements; payment is pay to unlock after the build; there's no "Send clicks
to" field; Run it again is Engine 8; Pause, Resume and the listing watcher are Engine 6.

## 9c. No links in Instagram posts (Adam, 22 Nov)

Instagram doesn't make links in post captions clickable, and a pasted URL looks broken. So every
post gets **two captions**: the Facebook one can carry the link to the listing page; **the
Instagram one never contains a link.** It ends with an invitation instead ("Comment TOUR and I'll
send you the details", "Message me for a private showing"), and Engine 6 flags those comments and
messages as leads. The checker rejects any Instagram caption with a URL in it. (Adam saw this
bug in BAP's Real Estate Boost test ads; the same rule should go into the Boost.)

## 9d. The image model (checked 22 Nov 2026)

- **Stay with OpenAI, on its newest image model.** OpenAI's image models lead the public
  rankings for overall quality, text on images and editing a real photo, which is exactly this job.
  BAP's Boost uses GPT Image 2.5 "Sunburst"; OpenAI released **GPT-Image-2.6** (Sunburst for
  quality, Flare for speed) on 4 Nov 2026, with more consistent edits. **Resmara's config still
  defaults to `gpt-image-1`, two generations old: switch it first** (Dan to confirm what's set on
  Render).
- **The model is a setting, not hard-wired,** so it can be swapped without a rebuild.
- **A bake-off before launch, then every quarter:** run the gold set (section 5a) through the
  current model and the strongest alternatives (Google's Nano Banana Pro, Ideogram, Recraft, FLUX);
  Adam picks by eye; switch only if one clearly wins on looks, lettering and keeping the photo
  untouched. Different models can be used for different jobs (for example one for posts, one for the
  ad) if that wins.
- **Cost:** measure the real image cost per campaign against the $49 before launch.

## 9e. What the build hands to the calendar (the end result)

When the build finishes, the campaign's calendar is fully populated, ready to review:

- **14 posts, one per day**, each with: the finished 4:5 design (or the listing video on day 1), the
  day and posting time, a title, **a Facebook caption** (with the listing page link) and **an
  Instagram caption** (no link), hashtags, the post type (photo, video, collage, carousel, question,
  note from the agent), the photo(s) used and where they came from, any "please check" facts, and a
  quality flag ("Needs a look") if the checks couldn't clear it.
- **The ad** as a bar across days 1–14: the static hook ads (4:5 and 9:16), the video ad if there's a
  video, the ad text, headline and button, where clicks go, and the budget ($50 default).
- **Report markers** on day 7 and day 14.
- **Launch details pre-filled:** go-live date (the next available day), the ad budget, and Run it
  until it sells (off by default).

## 9f. Also carried over from what Resmara does today (audit, 22 Nov)

Things the current engine or the specs already do that this brain keeps:

- **A mix of post types:** some days are multi-photo **carousel posts**, not just single images.
- **Quiet-zone tags** on every photo, so text is placed only in calm space.
- **The trend brief and style choices reach the design step** (today the style research never does:
  the brief gets one fixed layout line). This is the core fix.
- **Round 2 and later** (Run it until it sells, Engine 8): reads what earlier rounds used, so it never
  repeats a story angle or headline, leads with photos not used yet, and makes new ads.
- **Download a post** (exists today): kept, for agents posting themselves until their Page is connected.
- **Fair Housing on every caption and every agent edit, not just the ad** (Adam, 22 Nov): the same
  banned-phrase and compliance check runs on the plan, the hashtags and every edit; a non-compliant
  edit can't be saved and is never learned.

### Swapping a photo (exists today, improved)

Today an agent can open a post before they approve and upload a replacement photo. Two changes:

- **Pick from this home's photos first.** The swap opens the home's own photos (every photo Engine 3
  gathered), with "Upload your own" under them. Uploads are still resized and saved as JPEG.
- **The post is redesigned with the new photo.** Today the new photo replaces the finished design, so
  the headline, name, license number and phone are lost and a plain photo goes out. Now Resmara
  redraws that one post on the new photo, keeping its words, style and agent details, and runs the
  same checks (text, Fair Housing, Art Director) before it shows it.

### Hashtags: clean, consistent, competitive

Platform rules, baked into the rulebook and refreshed monthly with the other platform rules:

- **Instagram allows 5 hashtags at most** (enforced since December 2025). Today's code allows up to 6,
  which must be fixed. Resmara uses **3 to 5**.
- **Facebook: 0 to 2.** More reads as spam there.
- They go **at the end of the caption**, after a line break, the same way on every post.

What the set is made of (Instagram):

1. **One local tag:** the city or neighborhood (#ReddingHomes, #SouthReddingRealEstate).
2. **One niche real estate tag** for that market or type of home (#ReddingRealEstate, #ShastaCountyHomes).
3. **One tag for what this post shows** (#OpenHouseRedding, #MountainViewHome).
4. **The agent's own tag**, the same on every post they ever make (#SellingWithSwana). Set once from
   their brand kit and editable on My Brand.
5. **Optionally one trending tag** from the trend brief, only when it truly fits the post.

Rules:
- **No giant generic tags** (#realestate, #househunting, #dreamhome): posts drown in them. Prefer
  mid-sized and niche tags that people in that area actually follow.
- **No banned or broken tags.** Every tag is checked against a refreshed list before it's used.
- **Fair Housing applies to hashtags too:** nothing that describes who should live there
  (#FamilyFriendly, #PerfectForRetirees, #SafeNeighborhood).
- **Rotated, never identical:** the local and agent tags stay; the others change post to post so the
  14 posts don't all carry the same block.
- **It learns:** results show which tags bring reach in that market, and the trend brief keeps the
  list current. Shared per market, like the trend brief.

### Posting times and ad hours

- **Posts go out when that agent's audience is online most.** Resmara uses the agent's own audience
  data from Facebook and Instagram where Meta provides it; for new or small accounts, it uses what's
  working for agents in that market. Each campaign's results sharpen it. No more fixed 9 AM / 7 PM.
- **Posts are only scheduled between 6 AM and 11 PM** local time, at the best time inside that window.
- **The ad never runs between 11 PM and 6 AM.** This is a hard limit, fixed in code: every Resmara ad
  set runs on Meta's ad schedule with those hours switched off, so none of the agent's budget is spent
  overnight. Meta only allows an ad schedule with a lifetime budget, which Resmara already requires
  (section 9b). The hours follow the viewer's time zone, which is the agent's, given the local radius.
- The calendar shows both: each post's time, and "Ads run 6 AM to 11 PM" under the ad bar.

### If part of a build fails

What a failure can be: the image AI refuses or times out on a post; the checker rejects a post twice
(misspelled street name, wrong price, cut-off phone number); a photo turns out to be a broken file; or
the caption step returns nothing for a day.

What happens today: a post that fails design quietly goes out as the **plain listing photo**, with no
text and no agent details, and nobody is told. If the whole design step fails, all 14 posts quietly
become plain photos.

What Engine 4 does instead:
1. **Retry that post once.**
2. **If it fails again, use the fallback design:** Resmara's own layout over the photo, with the text
   drawn in code so it's always spelled right (section 9). Every post still has a finished design.
3. **If the photo itself is broken,** pick the next best photo of the home that isn't used yet.
4. **Flag it "Needs a look"** on the calendar so the agent can glance at it before approving.
5. **If more than 3 posts fail, or the ad fails,** the build retries itself before the agent sees it,
   and Adam gets an alert. The agent sees "Still finishing your campaign" rather than broken posts.
6. **Nothing is charged until pay to unlock,** so a failed build never costs the agent anything.

## 10. Decided with Adam (22 Nov 2026)

- Built for the agent who doesn't want to think; anyone can dial in.
- AI in every decision; never one template; unique per agent.
- Trend research, local and national, from real engagement numbers; learn, never copy.
- Post styles replace Modern / Classic / Rustic / Match my brand; no taste quiz before the first build.
- 👍 / 👎 on every post, always; the engine learns from ratings, edits and results.
- No "restyle all": they change individual posts if they want.
- The Your look picker comes off My Brand.
- Engine 4 builds the ad; Engine 6 launches it. The ad has its own rules: a true-to-the-house hook, minimal text, its own graphic, up to 3 hook versions tested, a hook library that learns, all placements incl. Marketplace, clicks to the Resmara listing page.
- Instagram captions never contain a link.
- With a video, the video ad and the static ads always run together.
- A platform rulebook (Meta ad standards, housing rules, Fair Housing, placement specs), refreshed monthly and on every rejection, guides every ad decision; an ad-policy check before launch; rejections fixed and resubmitted once.
- Learning never narrows into one look: references set the feel not the layout, rotate, a third of each campaign is new, no repeats.
- No two Resmara agents in the same market look alike.
- Quality never depends on feedback: rulebook, Art Director check, checker, tuned to Adam's gold set.
- The 14-day arc in section 6.
- 👎 needs no reason: the AI compares 👎 and 👍 posts' features to find what's causing it.
- Name, license number and phone on every post (graphic or caption); how they're shown varies and is learned.
- The image AI designs the posts (the coolest result), kept consistent by their 👍 posts as references, the fixed footer and the checker; the hybrid stays as the fallback.
- Calendar gets a Gallery view.
- Stay with OpenAI's newest image model (GPT-Image-2.6 Sunburst), as a swappable setting, with a bake-off before launch and every quarter.
- The build ends with the calendar fully populated (section 9e).
- Carried over: ask for a missing phone, license or brokerage in the campaign flow; the post writer
  reads the Voice of the client.
- Hashtags: Instagram 3 to 5 (never over Meta's cap of 5), Facebook 0 to 2, at the end of the caption;
  local + niche + post tag + the agent's own tag (+ one trending tag only if it fits); no giant generic
  or banned tags; Fair Housing applies; rotated; learned per market (section 9f).
- Posts go out when the agent's audience is online most, only between 6 AM and 11 PM.
- Ads never run between 11 PM and 6 AM: a hard limit on every ad set, using Meta's ad schedule.
- Photo swap picks from the home's photos first and redesigns the post with the new photo.
- A failed post retries, then gets the fallback design and a "Needs a look" flag; never a plain photo.
